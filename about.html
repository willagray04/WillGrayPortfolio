About

Seeing a project from more than one side

I am a Texas A&M student finishing a degree in Geographic Information Science and Technology in December 2026, with a minor in Leadership in the Design and Construction Professions.

My interest in land development grew out of seeing projects from several different positions. GIS taught me to evaluate land technically. My construction minor and field work taught me what it takes to actually build something. Two summers in civil engineering showed me how entitlements, infrastructure, and permitting decide the direction of a project long before anything happens on site. Working directly for a developer showed me the same project from the owner’s side, where cost, feasibility, and risk are weighed against each other.

What ties those together is that I like being the person who can move between the spatial data, the cost, and the decision — not just hand one off to the next group.

What I have worked on

  • Land development and entitlements. Two summers interning at Gray Engineering in Austin, supporting site plan preparation, permitting coordination, and municipal submittals; reviewing construction documents; pre-bid quantity takeoffs; contractor coordination; and construction inspection.
  • Real estate development. A 2026 internship with Service Group in Austin, shadowing the Vice President of Real Estate Development across site evaluation, deal structuring, and project planning on active projects.
  • Construction estimating. A 2026 internship with Sendero Homes, where I developed an automated construction cost estimation tool to streamline budget creation and project cost analysis.
  • Underground utility construction. A summer at Platinum Underground in Georgetown, in the field on utility construction and project operations.
  • GIS and spatial analysis. Coursework and project work in ArcGIS Pro, hazard and exposure mapping, thematic cartography, GNSS control, and Python-based spatial analysis.

The work I want

I am looking for work in land and real estate development, construction management, and GIS technology — ideally somewhere those overlap rather than sit in separate departments. The roles that interest me most involve evaluating a property and working out not only what can be built on it, but whether building it makes sense.

Longer term I want responsibility for evaluating and executing projects: identifying an opportunity, testing whether it is feasible, assembling the team, and seeing it through.

How I work

I am comfortable being the person who goes and gets the data. On the hazard mapping project I am the team contact and handle data acquisition, which means tracking down FEMA, Census, and infrastructure sources and getting them into a usable state before anyone can analyse anything. That part is unglamorous and it decides whether the rest of the project works.

I also lean on automation where it earns its place. Scripting map production meant different hazard weightings could be compared quickly instead of being redrawn by hand, and building an estimating tool meant budgets could be checked against real past jobs rather than assembled from scratch each time.
