Multi-Hazard Exposure and Population Risk Mapping in Brazos County, Texas

Which census tracts in Brazos County face the highest risk of natural hazard exposure — wildfire, extreme heat, and flooding — and how much danger are critical infrastructure and major population centers truly in?

The problem

Natural hazards such as wildfire, extreme heat, and flooding pose growing risks to communities across Texas, but that risk is rarely distributed evenly within a single county. Brazos County — home to Bryan–College Station and Texas A&M University — is exposed to all three.

The data that exists already reports them separately. FEMA’s National Risk Index gives a score for wildfire, a score for extreme heat, and a score for flooding, but never one comparable number. That makes it hard to answer the question a planner or emergency manager actually has: across the whole county, where is risk concentrated, and who and what is sitting in it?

My role

I am the team contact and project lead, and I am responsible for data acquisition. That means coordinating the schedule, submitting the proposal, and sourcing the three FEMA hazard layers along with the population and infrastructure data for Brazos County census tracts — getting all of it into a state the rest of the team can actually analyse.

Data and method

Data

  • FEMA National Risk Index — hazard scores for wildfire, extreme heat, and flooding at the census-tract level for Brazos County, Texas.
  • U.S. Census / ACS — population data, used to quantify how many people sit inside each risk band.
  • Critical infrastructure locations — hospitals, schools, and emergency services, from a source such as HIFLD or Brazos County GIS open data.

Method

Using Python — pandas and geopandas — the three hazard layers are normalized onto a comparable scale and combined into a single composite risk score for each census tract. Multiple weighting schemes are then tested to find the most representative composite value, rather than assuming the three hazards should count equally.

Because testing weightings by hand would mean redrawing maps over and over, map production itself is scripted. That turns a weighting change into a re-run instead of an afternoon of cartography, and makes the different scenarios directly comparable.
